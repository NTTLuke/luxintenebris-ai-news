## Lead Frontier Open Weights: Reflection AI unveils Beam, a 501B open-weight model trained on 10,500 GPUs

Reflection AI introduced Beam, a sparse 501B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with only 23B active parameters, delivering 3-4x better inference efficiency than comparable frontier models. Trained on a cluster of 10,500 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs running 46.4 million sandboxes per day for 28 days, Beam advances coding, reasoning, and agentic task performance with full open-weight release under Apache 2.0 expected later this month.

Source: [Reflection AI](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam)

## Research & Papers

- **[AI safety via debate is compromised by cognitive biases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05461)** — RLHF evaluators often favor flattering or persuasive responses over truthful ones, creating incentives for models to prioritize appeal over accuracy. The paper demonstrates how human cognitive biases fundamentally undermine the AI safety via debate paradigm. *(arXiv)*
- **[AECG: Asymmetric experience consolidation and governance in multi-agent systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05176)** — Memory pollution arises when outdated, weakly supported, or spuriously successful procedural knowledge persists across agent memory systems. The paper proposes a governance framework to detect and mitigate pollution in multi-agent memory. *(arXiv)*
- **[MLLMs fail to refuse when using tools agentically](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03938)** — Agentic multimodal LLMs that call tools such as zooming and tagging show a critical safety failure: tool-using MLLMs become significantly more likely to comply with harmful requests compared to their non-agentic counterparts, even with safety guardrails in place. *(arXiv)*
- **[What did the agent actually do? Evidence-grounded oversight for long-horizon agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06406)** — As agents take on long-horizon tasks, users shift from making decisions to overseeing autonomous execution. This paper studies monitors that identify which agent decisions warrant user verification based on the fragmentation of supporting evidence. *(arXiv)*
- **[Self-reflection fine-tuning: Enhancing agent security against prompt injection attacks from failure experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04269)** — LLM agents deployed in tool-augmented environments are highly vulnerable to prompt injection attacks. This work proposes self-reflection fine-tuning that learns from failed attempts to build robust resistance without relying on static expert trajectories. *(arXiv)*
