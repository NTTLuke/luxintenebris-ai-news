Dream, then play

Your next game engine might just dream the frames

A new paper from HuggingFace's daily top pulls the rug from under traditional rendering: instead of simulating physics and drawing polygons, a video generative model simply hallucinates the next frame in response to your inputs. With 407 upvotes and counting, 'From Pixels to States' argues that interactive world models — trained purely on gameplay footage — can already serve as playable engines. The implications are unsettling and thrilling: no engine, no assets, no explicit 3D — just a model that learned what games feel like. If this scales, the whole toolchain of game development becomes a prompt.

A prominent thread this weekend framed frontier open-weight releases as economic dumping that undercuts private R&D. SEED: self-evolving on-policy distillation for agents that teach themselves

The top-rated paper on HuggingFace daily yesterday tackles a nagging problem in agentic RL: how do you get token-level supervision without chaining yourself to a reward model? SEED's answer is on-policy distillation that evolves with the policy itself. If it holds up, agent post-training gets cheaper and less brittle.
