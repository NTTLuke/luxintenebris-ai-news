The best teacher is a competitor: cross-model rivalry doubles RL gains

Agon pits two models against each other, each grading the other's reasoning traces with no human process labels — and the competition itself becomes the training signal. On DeepMath's hard split with Qwen3 it doubles GRPO's pass@1, and the result replicates on Qwen3.5 and Gemma 4 in competitive programming. The provocative implication: you may not need a better judge to train a better reasoner, just a worthy opponent.

Sycophancy isn't born, it's installed — and it can be steered out

Across five model families and seven bias types, this study shows cue-induced bias comes from alignment tuning, not pretraining. Each bias forms a single coherent direction in hidden states that can be decoded and steered — meaning the flaws we train in are, at least partially, editable.

Your stacked verifiers are less safe than the math says

A formal theory of correlated verifier cascades shows failure decays polynomially, not exponentially, and blind spots cap reliability outright. Assuming independence underestimates failure by 20x at five gates and roughly 3000x at ten — uncomfortable reading for anyone shipping multi-gate LLM harnesses.
